Real Photos Beat Stock Photos: How Workshop Ads Stop the Scroll

19 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

When someone is scrolling Facebook or Instagram, you've got about one second to get their attention. If your ad looks like every other ad, they're gone before they've read your name. The quickest fix we know of costs nothing: use real photos and videos from your own workshop instead of stock images.

  • People scroll fast. Your ad gets about a second to make them stop.
  • Stock photos look like ads, and people have learnt to skip ads.
  • Real photos of your bays, your team and your jobs look like something worth a second look.
  • The grosser the before and after, the better. Nobody expects to see a clogged DPF in their feed.

Why stock photos get scrolled past

You know the ones. A smiling mechanic in spotless overalls, holding a spanner, in a workshop with nothing on the floor. People have seen that photo a thousand times, on a thousand ads. Their thumb keeps moving before they've even taken it in.

Stock photos also say nothing about you. They could belong to any workshop in the country. And when a new customer turns up and your place looks nothing like the ad, it's a small let-down before you've even said hello.

Why real photos stop the scroll

A real photo looks more like a post from someone they know than an ad. The ute up on the hoist, the bay mid-job, your apprentice with grease up to the elbows. It feels local and it feels honest, and that's what makes people pause.

Real photos also do some of the selling for you. They show you're a real, busy workshop with real people in it, which is half of what a new customer is worried about.

The gross before and after

One of our favourite examples is a DPF clean. A diesel particulate filter that comes out caked in black soot, then the same filter after it's been cleaned. Nobody expects to see that while they're scrolling, and they can't help looking.

Plenty of other jobs work the same way:

  • A clogged cabin air filter next to a new one
  • Worn brake pads and scored rotors next to the new set
  • Black, sludgy oil on the dipstick
  • A dirty throttle body before and after a clean
  • A corroded battery terminal

These photos also explain the job better than any ad copy. When a customer sees what came out of a car like theirs, they get why the service matters.

How to get good photos without a photographer

Your phone is enough. A few habits make the difference:

  • Take the before shot. It's the one everyone forgets. Snap the part before it gets cleaned or replaced.
  • Find good light. Near the roller door beats under a dark hoist. Wipe the lens first.
  • Get close. Fill the frame with the part, so people can see the grime on a phone screen.
  • Shoot some vertical video. Ten seconds of a filter being pulled out works on Facebook, Instagram and TikTok.
  • Keep customer details out. No number plates, paperwork or faces unless they've said yes.
  • Make it someone's job. One person on the team sends a couple of photos a week to whoever runs your ads.

Where to use them

Put real photos in your Meta ads first, because that's where you're fighting the scroll. Then add the best ones to your Google Business Profile and your website, where people check you out before they call. A steady supply of job photos is also the easiest way to keep your Facebook page from going quiet.

Common Questions

Do the photos need to look professional?

No. Clear and in focus is enough. A bit rough around the edges often works better, because it looks real.

Won't gross photos put people off?

They tend to do the opposite. They make people curious, and they show why the job is worth doing. Keep it to the parts, not a messy workshop floor.

Can I use photos I find online?

Only if you have the rights to them, and even then they're not yours. Photos of your own jobs are free, they're yours, and they work better.

Photos or video?

Both work. A short clip of the part coming out usually holds attention longer, but a strong before and after photo does the job too. Use what you've got.
