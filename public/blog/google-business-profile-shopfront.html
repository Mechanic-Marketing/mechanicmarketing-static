Your Google Business Profile Is Your Shopfront: What Customers Check First

12 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

When someone's car starts making a strange noise, they don't go looking for your website. They pull out their phone and search "mechanic near me". The first thing that comes up is a map with a handful of workshops on it. That listing is your Google Business Profile, and for a lot of customers it's the first look they get at your workshop. Sometimes it's the only one.

  • Your Google Business Profile is the free listing that shows up on Google Maps and in the map results when people search for a mechanic nearby.
  • Plenty of customers decide from that listing alone, before they ever reach your website.
  • Services, hours, photos, reviews and regular updates: the more of it you fill in, the better your chance of showing up above the workshops around you.
  • It costs nothing. Most of the work is one afternoon to set it up properly, then a few minutes here and there to keep it fresh.

What a Google Business Profile actually is

It's the box that shows your workshop's name, star rating, opening hours, phone number, photos and a button for directions. It appears in Google Maps and in the map results near the top of a local search. You set it up and manage it for free at business.google.com. If you've heard it called Google My Business, that's the old name for the same thing.

Why it often matters more than your website

Think about how people pick a mechanic when they need one now. They see a few workshops on the map, compare the stars, check who's open, glance at the photos and tap "Call". Lots of them never click through to a website at all.

That makes your profile your shopfront. If it's half filled in, with no photos and the wrong hours, it's like a workshop with the roller door half down. People drive past.

What customers check in the first few seconds

  • Your stars and how many reviews you have. A 4.8 from 200 reviews looks safer than a 5.0 from three.
  • Whether you're open. Wrong hours cost you calls, and annoy anyone who turns up to a locked gate.
  • Your photos. Real shots of your workshop and team tell people you're a real, busy business.
  • What you work on. Someone with a diesel ute or a European car looks for it in your services before they call.
  • How recent it all is. New reviews, owner replies and fresh photos say someone is looking after the place.

Fill in every box

Google ranks local results on three things: how relevant you are to the search, how far you are from the person searching, and how well known you are. You can't move your workshop closer to every customer. You can do a lot about the other two, and most of it comes down to filling the profile in properly.

Services. List every job you want to be found for: logbook servicing, brakes, AC regas, roadworthies, diesel, whatever you do. If it isn't on there, Google has less reason to show you for it.

Categories. Pick the closest match as your main category, usually "Auto repair shop", then add your specialities as extra categories.

Hours. Keep them right, including public holidays. With Christmas coming, set your holiday hours now so nobody turns up to a closed workshop.

Photos. Add the front of the workshop so people can find you, plus the team, the bays and finished jobs. Clear phone photos are fine.

Reviews. Ask every happy customer, and reply to every review, good or bad. Here's how to ask for 5 star reviews without it feeling awkward.

Updates. Post an offer, a seasonal reminder or a quick tip now and then. It shows up on your listing and tells people you're active.

Keep it up to date

A profile isn't a set-and-forget job. Hours change, photos go stale and services get added. We've put together a quarterly Google Business Profile checklist you can run to keep yours in shape.

Common Questions

Is a Google Business Profile free?

Yes. It costs nothing to set up or to keep. Ads can appear on Google Maps too, but the profile itself is free.

I'm a mobile mechanic. Can I still have one?

Yes. You can hide your address and list the suburbs you cover instead. Google calls this a service-area business.

Why don't I show up on the map when I search for myself?

Where you search from changes the results a lot, because distance is one of the three things Google looks at. Search from a suburb your customers live in, not from your own workshop. A thin profile and only a few reviews will also hold you back.

Do I still need a website?

Yes. Lots of people check your profile first, then click through to your site to see what you do or to book. The two work together.
